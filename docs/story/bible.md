## 1. The premise in one paragraph

The city runs on paper: newspapers, telegrams, contracts, confessions, ballots. For three years, somebody has been editing it.
The organization is called **the Lexicon**. Its members give up their names and are issued a single five-letter word, their
**headword**. To the Lexicon, that word *is* the person. It's how they're paid, ordered and, when necessary, erased. Detective
Dash Lexington spends one long night per chapter hunting a Lexicon member before 6:00 AM, then goes home (or doesn't) to a father who's
dying, a wife who's leaving, and a nephew drifting toward trouble. In the winter of 1949 he tells all of it to a grand jury. Above it all sits
**the Editor**, whose voice calls Dash in the dark and seems to know every word before Dash does. The Editor pours Dash's drinks. He's
known Dash since they were eleven.

## 2. The organization: the Lexicon

**Structure.** The Lexicon is organised like a dictionary, and its members use the vocabulary straight-faced.

| Rank | Title | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Top | **The Editor** | Decides what the city reads, hears, signs and believes. Members have never seen the Editor. Orders arrive as corrections in red pencil. |
| Second | **The Proofreader** | The Editor's right hand. "Corrects errors": removes people who become problems. (Chapter 10's culprit.) |
| Members | **Entries** | Specialists: typesetters, notaries, announcers, forgers. Each one owns one part of the city's paperwork. |
| Outside | **Footnotes** | Hired help who never learn the Lexicon exists: dockhands, clerks, numbers runners, the Varga family's leftovers. |

**How it uses words.** Every order is hidden in plain sight in the city's own text: a misprinted headline, a radio announcer's
deliberate stumble, a classified ad with one wrong letter, a telegram that costs a cent too much. The Lexicon's master book,
**the Index**, maps every headword to a real identity and every member's crimes. It's the one document that could hang them all.
**(new in r2)** The Index is built on the Professor's pulped dictionary. The Editor bought the only surviving manuscript in 1946, and
every headword is drawn from its pages. That's why every road seems to lead back to the Professor.

**Why every member is "a word on the run"** (the fiction for a random answer):
- Joining the Lexicon means giving up your name. You are issued a **headword**, five letters long, drawn from the Index.
  Inside the Lexicon you have no other name.
- The Lexicon's oath: **if the law ever speaks your headword to your face, you are finished.** Your file is "struck", your protection
  ends, and the Proofreader comes for you. So when Dash names the word, the culprit surrenders rather than run from both sides.
  That's why catching the word *is* catching the person.
- Whenever a member is exposed or flees, the Index **reissues their headword**: a new word, drawn blind. So the word is different
  every night, every retry and every replay. Writers never know the word. Scenes must work for any five letters.
- Dash's method stays the same as in Random Case: he brings in candidate words ("suspects"). Letters with alibis walk out gray, letters
  "in the gang" turn yellow, and letters in the right place at the right time turn green.

**Its history.** The Lexicon rose in 1946 by quietly replacing the Varga crime family. The Vargas broke bones; the Lexicon rewrites
records. Several Varga leftovers now work as Footnotes, and they hate it. (This gives writers a source of informants and red herrings.)

## 3. The Editor: Sal (new in r2)

**Who he is:** **Salvatore "Sal" Bruno**, owner and only bartender of **The Last Word**. Dash's best friend since 1929, when they were eleven
and ran the waterfront together. They served together in the war (Sal pulled Dash out of a river in Italy and never mentions it).
Sal is big, calm and gentle. He polishes clean glasses, and he is never wrong. The whole precinct trusts him with secrets, and that's
exactly the point.

**The wound (1931):** at thirteen, Dash and Sal broke into a waterfront warehouse on a dare. A lantern went over, the warehouse burned, and the
night watchman died. Dash's father, Patrick "Pop" Lexington, was the arresting officer. To save his own son, Pop put it all on Sal. Sal did
three years in reform school and never said Dash's name. Dash has told himself for eighteen years that he doesn't remember that night clearly.
The record followed Sal everywhere: no promotion in the army, no liquor licence in his own name (the Last Word is licensed to a dead cousin),
no bank loan, no clean slate. *The city wrote me down once,* he tells Dash in the finale, *and it never let me change a word.*

**Motive:** if the record decides who you are, then whoever holds the pen owns the city. Sal built the Lexicon from people the record had
erased: ex-cons, disgraced clerks, forgers, widows with nothing. His endgame is **the Final Edition**: one morning on which the city's record
(court files, deeds, the election count, the front page) is replaced with his corrected version. Somewhere in that new record, a boy named
Salvatore Bruno never set foot in that warehouse.

**What he feels about Dash:** love, and eighteen years of waiting for Dash to say "it was me". Sal protects Dash throughout. The Proofreader
has standing orders never to touch him, which explains a few lucky escapes. Sal feeds Dash real leads, because he wants to be caught by the
one person who owes him the truth, and because he can't stand to watch Dash lose.

**How the Editor taunts Dash** (the `WORD` phone voice and red eyes from Random Case):
- **Phone calls**, always when Dash is alone, often just before the deadline. The voice speaks in definitions it learned from the Professor's
  dictionary: *"Lexington. Proper noun. A small town where a war began. Also: a man about to lose one."* It's disguised, and never sounds like Sal.
- **Red-pencil corrections**: Dash finds his own notes "edited" overnight.
- **The Editor always knows the stakes:** the voice names the night's deadline before anyone tells Dash.

**Fair-play clues pointing at Sal** (at most one per chapter, each deniable on its own; never letter-dependent):
- He's never wrong, because he already knows how things end.
- He pours Dash's drink before Dash orders, including the night Dash orders something different.
- The Editor calls the **Last Word's payphone** while Sal is "in the back".
- Sal keeps a red pencil behind his ear for the tabs.
- Sal knows a detail of the night's case before it's in the papers ("Heard it on the radio." The radio's been broken for a week.)
- The bar is called *The Last Word*. Nobody remarks on it. Ever.
- Sal never once asks Dash who the culprit was. He only asks what time Dash got home.

**The red herring: the Professor** (Ambrose Thackeray, lexicographer). His pulped dictionary is the Index; the Editor's phone voice uses his
definitions; in chapter 10 the Proofreader's orders turn up in his handwriting (forged by the Ghostwriter on the Editor's orders). He is
innocent, and genuinely helpful as an informant. He never lies about the odds. He has suspected for a year that someone bought his manuscript
and has been too proud to say so. In the good endings he testifies; in the bad ones he's ruined by suspicion.

## 4. The frame: the record of Dash Lexington

**Where:** a grand jury hearing room at City Hall, February 1949. Snow outside, radiators knocking. The inquiry is into "the
Lexicon affair". Dash testifies for ten sessions, one night per session.

**Who types it:** **Ruth Abernathy** (`RUTH`, new), the court stenographer. Fifties, unflappable, faster than anyone alive, and she
misses nothing. She speaks rarely, and only in short dry lines. She is the audience for Dash's narration and the player's stand-in.

**How it's used:**
- Each chapter's **opening** starts in the hearing room. Ruth threads paper, the session number is typed (`~paper`), and Dash begins.
  Then a cut to the night itself.
- Each chapter's **outro** returns to the room for a line or two before the hook, then the **interlude** (§7) plays: Dash's life the
  next day, told to Ruth more quietly. *"That's not in the record, Detective."* / *"Put it in anyway."*
- **Losing a chapter (D1) is a retelling.** After the escape, the room again: Ruth stops typing. *"Detective, that isn't what you told
  the papers."* Dash: *"Strike that. That's not how it went."* Ruth pulls the sheet, and the night starts over. Because the Index reissues
  headwords, the culprit's word is new, and the retry's scenes are different by design (D1's retry picker).
- **The endings** are the grand jury's finding on the record, plus what became of Dash's life (§8).
- **Easter-egg seeds live in the frame** (§9). Ruth's lines are the cleanest place to plant deniable observations.

## 6. The ten chapters

Per-chapter script vars (T6): `{chapterNo} {chapterTitle} {culprit} {alias} {crime} {deadline}`. `{culprit}` is the culprit's real name,
`{alias}` is their Lexicon title ("the Typesetter"), and the **headword is the answer** (`{ANSWER}`), never known to writers.

**Midpoint turn:** every chapter has one, delivered by the chapter's **guess-3 core scenes** (all buckets carry it, flavoured by how the
hunt is going). **Outro hooks** are the 4 outro beats (fast catch / slow catch / near miss / escaped) from T8's budget. After the outro comes
the chapter's **interlude** (§7).

### Chapter 1: "Stop the Presses"
- **Culprit:** Linus Pell, *the Typesetter*. Fussy, quotes the style manual, irons his shirts at the office.
- **Crime:** Murdered the *Gazette*'s night editor, who noticed Pell setting coded headlines.
- **M.O.:** Hides orders in typos. One wrong letter in a headline tells a Lexicon member who to silence next.
- **Deadline (6:00 AM):** **the presses roll.** The morning edition carries a coded headline marking a witness for death.
- **Opening:** the hearing room, session one. Then midnight: Vera calls Dash from the proof desk. "There's a typo on page one that isn't a typo."
- **Midpoint turn:** the coded headline isn't about the witness. It's a *job posting*. The Lexicon is hiring.
- **Catch:** the presses are stopped; Vera holds the plate in her inky hands. **Escape:** the paper runs; the witness is found at dawn.
- **Outro hook:** the plate's misprint spells out a place: *last call*. The Last Word.
- **Arcs moved:** Vera (first time they've worked together in years), Pete (spots the misprint), Lola (cameo: hires Dash for something else, "next week").

### Chapter 2: "Last Call"
- **Culprit:** Della Marsh, *the Bookkeeper*. Warm, motherly, keeps a pencil behind each ear.
- **Crime:** Runs the Lexicon's ledger out of the Last Word's back room; poisoned a Varga Footnote who tried to sell it.
- **M.O.:** Bar tabs as accounts. Every unpaid tab is a payment.
- **Deadline:** **the freighter *Lindqvist* sails from Pier {pier} at 6:00** with a copy of the ledger.
- **Opening:** Sal, polishing a clean glass, finally says the word *Lexicon* out loud. "They use my back room, Dash. I let them. I'm sorry."
  **(r2: this confession is Sal's cover.** It's true, and it's small, and it makes him look like a victim.)
- **Midpoint turn:** Pete's tab is in the ledger. He's been a Footnote for a year without knowing it.
- **Catch:** the ledger copy is pulled off the gangway. **Escape:** the ship clears the breakwater; Sal pours one for the river.
- **Outro hook:** the ledger lists a payment to a prosecutor, for a confession signed by a man scheduled to die at dawn tomorrow.
- **Arcs moved:** Sal (the "confession"; he becomes an informant), Pete (the debt gag turns real).

### Chapter 3: "Dead Man's Sentence"
- **Culprit:** Augustin "Gus" Fairweather, *the Notary*. Courtly, sentimental, cries at weddings he's forged.
- **Crime:** Forged Eddie Ruiz's murder confession, notarised in his best hand.
- **M.O.:** Signatures, seals, sworn statements. If it's stamped, people believe it.
- **Deadline:** **Eddie Ruiz goes to the chair at 6:00** at State Penitentiary. Only the real Notary's arrest gets a stay.
- **Opening:** Fenn: "The dead man didn't die the way the confession says. Confessions don't usually get the knife on the wrong side."
- **Midpoint turn:** the confession's seal was bought from the precinct's own evidence locker. Somebody inside helped.
- **Catch:** the governor's call; Eddie walks out at sunrise. **Escape:** the Notary vanishes, but Fenn's evidence alone wins Eddie a stay
  at 5:58. Eddie lives in both branches (so his ally arc holds); the Notary is free (story flag `notary_free`).
- **Outro hook:** the Notary's last appointment book: *WKRN, studio B, 4 AM*.
- **Arcs moved:** Briggs (the leak points at his precinct), new ally Eddie. A boy taking the fall for someone else's crime will echo later. Don't underline it.

### Chapter 4: "Dead Air"
- **Culprit:** Celeste Avery, *the Announcer*. Velvet voice, terrible manners off-air, two packs a day.
- **Crime:** Strangled her engineer with a microphone cable when he noticed she was reading code.
- **M.O.:** Late-night dedications are orders. Sponsor names are targets.
- **Deadline:** **the 6:00 AM news broadcast.** The script contains the activation phrase for a Lexicon courier.
- **Opening:** a radio hiss in Dash's apartment. The dedication is to *Dash Lexington, from an old friend*.
- **Midpoint turn:** Mags Delgado, the station's relief engineer, has been logging the dedications for months, and she wants in.
- **Catch:** Mags pulls the plug at 5:59, and the city wakes to dead air. **Escape:** the phrase goes out; somewhere a courier picks up a suitcase.
- **Outro hook:** the activation phrase is a timetable: the 6:00 train, tomorrow.
- **Arcs moved:** new ally Mags; Dash and Vera (she hears the dedication too, and asks who the old friend is. He says he doesn't know.)

### Chapter 5: "Express"
- **Culprit:** Tomas Brandt, *the Courier*. Polite, punctual, wears gloves indoors.
- **Crime:** Threw a Pullman porter off the night freight; carries the **Index** itself.
- **M.O.:** Never the same suitcase, never the same name on the ticket.
- **Deadline:** **the 6:00 train out of Union Station** (the series' signature deadline, used straight).
- **Opening:** Nickel at his shoeshine stand: "Man in gloves tipped me a dollar. Nobody tips a dollar unless they're leaving forever."
- **Midpoint turn:** the suitcase holds half the Index, a typed copy of a dictionary manuscript. Dash recognises the Professor's style of definition.
- **Catch:** cuffs on the platform, steam everywhere, half the Index in evidence (story flag `index_half`). **Escape:** the train pulls out; Nickel runs alongside, then stops.
- **Outro hook:** the recovered half lists a target: the Hall of Records, *to be corrected by fire*.
- **Arcs moved:** Nickel (first real case of his own), Dooley (does the running, gets the praise, wants the shield), the Professor (suspicion begins).

### Chapter 6: "Short Fuse"
- **Culprit:** Wendell Pike, *the Full Stop*. Ex-army demolitions, gentle, keeps canaries.
- **Crime:** Planted a charge under the Hall of Records to burn the city's original deeds and court files.
- **M.O.:** Every job ends in a period: something that can't be undone.
- **Deadline:** **the fuse is timed for 6:00.**
- **Opening:** Briggs, three fingers up: "Three buildings evacuated. Three hours of sleep. Three reasons I'm not in the mood."
- **Midpoint turn:** the bomb is real, but the records were already moved out last week. The fire would hide that they're *gone*.
  (r2: one file was pulled separately and never moved: juvenile court, 1931. Nobody remarks on it.)
- **Catch:** the fuse is cut. **Escape:** the charge goes off, but Dooley clears the night clerk out first and is **wounded** in the blast
  (story flag `dooley_hurt`; Dooley is hurt in both branches, worse on an escape).
- **Outro hook:** the moved records went to a bank vault, booked under a forger's name.
- **Arcs moved:** Dooley (wounded), Briggs (blamed by City Hall for the evacuation).

### Chapter 7: "Wire Transfer"
- **Culprit:** Mirabel Quist, *the Forger*. Brilliant, bored, paints watercolours of banks.
- **Crime:** Forged deeds to the city's moved records, and the wire transfer that sells them.
- **M.O.:** Perfect paper. Her only tell is that she can't resist signing her work somewhere hidden.
- **Deadline:** **the Federal Reserve wire window opens at 6:00.** The sale clears and the city's records legally belong to a shell company.
- **Opening:** Briggs is suspended (the leak, the bomb, the papers). Dash works the case without a badge, out of Sal's back room.
- **Midpoint turn:** Mags decodes the precinct's dispatch log. **Penny Ashcroft**, the night dispatcher, is the leak. Briggs is clean.
- **Catch:** the transfer is frozen and Penny is arrested; Briggs is reinstated (story flag `records_saved`). **Escape:** the transfer clears and Briggs stays suspended
  until chapter 9 (story flags `briggs_out`, `penny_free`).
- **Outro hook:** Quist's hidden signature is on one more document: a ferry ticket, booked for Vera Lexington.
- **Arcs moved:** Briggs (suspicion → cleared), the mole thread closes, Vera (danger begins).

### Chapter 8: "Next of Kin"
- **Culprit:** Silas Grey, *the Ghostwriter*. Writes other people's letters, speeches and suicide notes. Soft-spoken, wears borrowed clothes.
- **Crime:** Kidnapped Vera, who found the Lexicon's ballot plan in the *Gazette*'s proofs.
- **M.O.:** Makes people disappear on paper first: forged farewell letters, resignations, tickets.
- **Deadline:** **the 6:00 ferry to Blackwell Island** carries Vera, along with a "farewell letter" in her handwriting that Grey wrote.
- **Opening:** the hearing room. Dash can't start. Ruth waits. Then: *"Session eight. My wife."*
- **Midpoint turn:** Vera's farewell letter has a proofreader's mark in it, a message only Dash would read: she isn't on the ferry yet.
  She's being held at the *Gazette*.
- **Catch:** Dash finds Vera before the ferry. **Escape:** Vera saves herself (she always could) and walks into the precinct at 7 AM, furious that he
  was late (story flag `vera_saved_herself`).
- **Outro hook:** the ballot plan: the count certifies at 6:00 the next morning, and the Lexicon has a woman inside the count.
- **Arcs moved:** Vera, Dash. (r2: Sal is the first person Dash calls after, and Sal is already on his way. Nobody told him.)

### Chapter 9: "The Count"
- **Culprit:** **Lola Vance**, *the Understudy*. Charming, theatrical, has been playing Dash since chapter 1.
- **Crime:** Swapped ballot boxes in the election warehouse; shot a poll watcher who recognised her.
- **M.O.:** Plays whatever part the job needs: client, widow, volunteer.
- **Deadline:** **the election count certifies at 6:00.** If the swapped boxes are counted, a Lexicon-owned slate takes City Hall.
- **Opening:** Lola, in Dash's office, in the same chair as chapter 1: "I need your help, detective. Again." She's lying. Again.
- **Midpoint turn:** Lola names the Proofreader, in exchange for a head start. It's a real name, which is the most frightening thing she's done.
  And, unprompted: "You're looking at the wrong old man, detective."
- **Catch:** the true boxes are counted, and Lola is cuffed mid-performance (story flag `lola_caught`). **Escape:** she bows and is gone; the count stands, contested.
- **Outro hook:** the Proofreader is "correcting" everyone who touched the count, and Mrs. Kowalski signed as a witness.
  (Kowalski is kept safe in both branches; the rent thread is resolved here: Dash finally pays.)
- **Arcs moved:** Lola (betrayal), Kowalski (rent paid; she calls him "Detective" for the first time), Briggs (back if `briggs_out`).

### Chapter 10: "Final Edition"
- **Culprit:** Ellery Thorne, *the Proofreader*. Courteous, colourless, carries a red pencil and a straight razor. Has removed eleven people.
- **Crime:** Every loose end of the series: the night editor, the engineer, the porter, and the witnesses.
- **M.O.:** Corrections. He never improvises.
- **Deadline:** **the Final Edition hits the streets at 6:00**: a counterfeit extra of the *Gazette* declaring the new city record. In the
  same hour, the Editor boards the 6:00 train.
- **Opening:** the WORD calls one last time and doesn't hang up. *"Last chance to proofread your life, Lexington."*
- **Midpoint turn:** the Proofreader's orders are in the Professor's handwriting. Dash sends Dooley to arrest the Professor at Union Station.
- **Catch / escape:** both end at 5:58. The Professor is on the platform, there to meet the Editor himself, having finally worked it out.
  How it ends is decided by the run (§8). In every ending, the last scene of the case is at the Last Word.
- **Outro:** none. Chapter 10 hands straight to the ending.
- **Arcs moved:** all of them.

## 7. Dash's life: the interludes (new in r2)

Between chapters, after each outro, the **interlude** shows Dash's life the next day: his father, his marriage, his sister and nephew,
and the bottle. These aren't case scenes. Nobody is caught, nothing is decoded. The tone shifts down: quieter, funnier in a sadder way,
fewer cut-ins (none, by rule), daylight sets where possible.

**How performance shapes them.** Every guess is an hour of the night. The chapter's winning attempt decides when Dash got home:

| Winning attempt | Dash got home | Interlude variant | Mark for the thread |
|---|---|---|---|
| guess 1–2 | before 2 AM | **kept**: he makes it, on time, present | 2 |
| guess 3–4 | 2–4 AM | **late**: he makes it, but tired, distracted, late | 1 |
| guess 5–6 | at dawn | **missed**: he doesn't make it, or makes it too late | 0 |
| any guess, but the chapter needed a retry (D1) | — | one step worse than the table above (a kept becomes late, a late becomes missed) | — |

So each interlude is written in **three variants** (kept / late / missed), all covering the same event. The marks are computed from
`campaign.results` (guesses and attempts per chapter), so no extra save data is needed. Each thread resolves in the endings by its total (§8).

### 7.1 Pop (his father): interludes after chapters 1, 5 and 8
**Patrick "Pop" Lexington**, 68, retired patrolman, failing heart, still shines his old shoes every morning. Dash and Pop haven't really talked
since Dash's mother's funeral. Pop has something he needs to say about 1931 before he dies, and Dash keeps not letting him.
- **After ch 1: "Visiting Hours".** Nora calls: Pop's had a heart attack. Kept: Dash is there when he wakes, and Pop makes a joke about the food.
  Late: Dash arrives as visiting hours end and talks to Pop through a closing door. Missed: Nora tells him Pop asked for him three times.
- **After ch 5: "The Good Shoes".** Pop is home, weak, and asks Dash to help him shine his shoes. He starts: "About the warehouse..." Kept: Dash lets him
  start, then a phone call interrupts. Late: Dash changes the subject. Missed: Dash isn't there, and Pop tells Tommy a story instead (Tommy repeats it badly later).
- **After ch 8: "Last Rites".** Pop is dying. Kept: Dash is at the bedside, and Pop confesses: he put the warehouse on Sal to save his own son. Late: Pop
  gets halfway, then a nurse comes. Missed: Pop dies before Dash arrives, and leaves a sealed letter Dash doesn't open yet.
  (The letter or the confession is what makes Dash understand Sal in the finale. With the letter, he opens it at the Last Word in ch 10.)

### 7.2 Vera (his marriage): interludes after chapters 2 and 6, plus the ch 8 rescue
Vera has been offered a copy desk at the *San Francisco Chronicle*. She'll go after the election. The question is whether Dash comes, and whether
she wants him to. She loves him; she's tired of being married to the night.
- **After ch 2: "Anniversary".** Their tenth. Dinner reserved at Luigi's, 8 PM. Kept: he's early, in a pressed suit, and she laughs for the first time in a year.
  Late: he arrives at dessert, and she's already paid. Missed: she eats alone and leaves the offer letter on his pillow.
- **After ch 6: "Two Tickets".** Vera buys two train tickets west, for after the election. Kept: Dash says he'll think about it, and means it.
  Late: he says "after this case". Missed: he finds one ticket on the table, and the other is gone.
- **Chapter 8's result also counts** toward this thread (the rescue: kept if guess 1–2, late if 3–4, missed if 5–6 or `vera_saved_herself`).

### 7.3 Nora and Tommy (his sister and nephew): interludes after chapters 3 and 7
**Nora**, 31, war widow, night switchboard operator. **Tommy**, 11, quick and lonely, idolizes his uncle Dash. He's started running slips for a
numbers man (a Lexicon Footnote, though nobody knows it). Dash at eleven, with Sal. The echo is the point.
- **After ch 3: "The Slips".** Nora finds betting slips in Tommy's coat and begs Dash to talk to him. Kept: Dash takes Tommy to the ballgame and talks
  to him like a man. Late: Dash lectures him on the stoop for five minutes, then gets a call. Missed: Dash sends money instead, and Tommy spends it on cigarettes.
- **After ch 7: "Runaway".** Tommy runs away after Nora announces she's marrying Walt. Kept: Dash finds him at the old waterfront warehouse site, where Dash
  and Sal used to play, and brings him home. Late: Nickel finds him and brings him to Dash. Missed: a patrolman picks Tommy up, the way Pop once picked up Sal.
  Tommy now has a record.

### 7.4 The bottle (Dash himself): interludes after chapters 4 and 9
Dash drinks: a little since the war, more since Vera stopped waiting up. He drinks at the Last Word, poured by his best friend.
- **After ch 4: "After Hours".** Closing time at the Last Word: Dash and Sal, and the war. Sal tells the river story for the first time, and almost says
  something about 1931 before he stops himself. Kept: Dash switches to coffee, and Sal looks oddly proud. Late: Dash has two and goes home. Missed: Dash
  closes the bar, and Sal walks him home and puts him to bed, like he has a hundred times.
- **After ch 9: "Last Call".** The night before the finale. Sal pours without asking. Kept: Dash pushes the glass back across the bar: "Not tonight, Sal.
  Not anymore." Late: he holds it a long time, then drinks. Missed: he drinks Sal's whole bottle, and Sal sits with him till dawn. Sal, quietly: "Whatever happens
  tomorrow, Dash, you're still my best friend." Every variant ends on that line.

### Interlude budget
Nine interludes × three variants = 27 short scenes (4–8 lines each). Each lives in the pack of the chapter it follows, with IDs like `c03.inter.kept`.
Writing rules: no cut-ins, no heavy lines, no case vars beyond `{chapterNo}`; family names, real places, daylight; the punchline lands on a sad note.

## 8. Endings (T7)

Each ending is two parts: **the case** (by band, as approved; D5/D7) and **the life**, four short codas (one per thread) chosen by that thread's
total marks from §7. A good run doesn't automatically mean a good life. A fast Dash usually gets home in time, but a single slow night can still cost him something.

### The case

| Ending | Condition | What happens to the Lexicon and Sal |
|---|---|---|
| **A: "Clean Copy"** | avg < 2.5 | Dash goes to the Last Word at dawn and finds Sal waiting with two coffees. Sal surrenders to his best friend, and they talk until the squad cars come. The Lexicon is broken. The Professor is cleared. Dooley gets his shield. |
| **B: "Errata"** | 2.5 – 3.5 | Sal is caught, but the Final Edition partly ran and some records are lost for good. Sal goes quietly; he won't look at Dash. |
| **C: "Margin Notes"** | 3.5 – 4.5 | The Lexicon is broken, but Sal is gone. The bar is dark, a glass is still wet. Postcards arrive for years, corrections in red pencil, unsigned. |
| **D: "Redacted"** | 4.5 – 5.5 | The city wins, Dash loses: his badge (the inquiry) or his marriage, shaped by flags. Sal is arrested by somebody else. Dash isn't told until it's in the papers. Dooley inherits the badge Dash lost (if he wasn't hurt), or leaves the force. |
| **Bad: "Last Train Out"** | avg ≥ 5.5 | Sal boards the 6:00 with the Final Edition under his arm. Dooley takes the fall for Dash's mistakes. The grand jury finds "insufficient record". Ruth types the last line and stops. |
| **Egg: "The Man in the Mirror"** | every chapter won on guess 1 of its first attempt | See §9. |

In A–D, Sal's last line to Dash (in person or on a postcard) is always about 1931, and how Dash answers depends on whether Pop told him (§7.1).

### The life (codas, read from §7 marks; each thread has three tiers)

A thread's tier comes from its total: **best** if it's at least two thirds of the maximum, **worst** if it's one third or less,
**middle** otherwise (Pop and Vera: best 4–6 · middle 3 · worst 0–2; Nora/Tommy and the bottle: best 3–4 · middle 2 · worst 0–1).
The easter egg replaces the codas: the perfect life is read back into the record as one more thing Dash wrote.

## 9. The easter egg: "The Man in the Mirror" (S5)

**What it means in play:** the player named every culprit's word on the first try, ten times, on the first attempt. In the fiction, Dash knew
every word before anyone told him. That's what a man who *wrote* the words would know. (It also means every interlude was "kept": the perfect
family life, which makes the reveal colder.)

**The reveal:** dawn, the Last Word. Sal is waiting with two coffees, as in ending A, but he doesn't surrender. He slides the red pencil across the
bar. *"You think I'm the Editor, Dash? I only pour. You always told me what was true."* Since 1931, Sal has carried Dash's crime. In 1946 Dash came to
him with an idea for fixing the record, and Sal built it, because Dash asked. Every headword Dash "guessed" on the first try was a word Dash chose.
Back in the hearing room, Ruth reads the record back: ten headwords, each named on the first try. *"Nobody is that good, Detective."* Dash:
*"Somebody is."* He confesses quietly and precisely, including the warehouse, and she types every word. Last line, Ruth: *"Spelled correctly."*

**Seeds** (at most one per chapter, always deniable, never letter-dependent):
- Dash names a culprit's *alias* a beat before anyone says it ("...the Typesetter. That's what they'd call him, anyway.")
- The WORD calls only when Dash is alone, and once while Dash is *already holding the receiver*, before it rings.
- Vera: "You talk in your sleep, Dash. Lists. Five letters at a time."
- Dash's home typewriter has a sticking key, and the Editor's red-pencil notes are typed with the same smudge. (Visual only, no letter named.)
- Ruth, in the frame: "You haven't asked what the record is for, Detective." / "You never pause before the names."
- Sal, never wrong: "You know what I like about you, Dash? You never look surprised."
- Pop (any variant of "The Good Shoes"): "You always did know how things would turn out, son. Even when you were small."

In normal endings each seed reads as noir texture, and the Sal clues (§3) cover them: of course the Editor seems close to Dash. Only the egg makes them add up.

## 10. Writing rules for story chapters

Everything in DEVELOPMENT.md T8 applies (budget, cut-in budget, any-guess scenes). In addition:

1. **Never depend on letters.** Use `{GUESS}`, `{hitsN}`, `{ANSWER}`... The headword is drawn fresh every attempt.
2. **The culprit is a person; the word is their headword.** Write "her headword", "the word she's hiding behind", never "the word was a noun".
3. **Every chapter's facts are fixed by this bible**: culprit, alias, crime, deadline, set pieces. Opening variants must all agree on them.
4. **Midpoint turns live in the guess-3 cores.** **Outro beats** set up the next chapter's hook (fast catch / slow catch / near miss / escaped).
5. **Interludes** (§7) come in three variants (kept / late / missed) of the same event, with no cut-ins and no case talk. Every variant is complete on its own.
6. **Sal is never written as sinister before the finale.** He's the warmest person in the book. The clues are facts, not tone.
7. **Story flags** (campaign-scoped, `~story name`), each set by one chapter's result: `notary_free` (3 escape), `index_half` (5 catch),
   `dooley_hurt` (6 escape), `briggs_out` and `penny_free` (7 escape), `records_saved` (7 catch), `vera_saved_herself` (8 escape), `lola_caught` (9 catch).
   Thread marks (§7) are computed from results, not flags.
8. **One easter-egg seed (§9) and one Sal clue (§3) per chapter, at most**, and only where they read naturally.
9. **No Random Case text.** Same characters, new lines. The checker enforces this.
10. **New locations** needed: the *Gazette* press room, State Penitentiary, WKRN studio, a freighter gangway, Hall of Records, bank vault,
    ferry slip, election warehouse, the grand jury hearing room, **(r2)** a hospital room, Pop's kitchen, Luigi's restaurant, the ballpark
    bleachers, and the burned-out waterfront warehouse. Each is a new file in `art/sets/`.
